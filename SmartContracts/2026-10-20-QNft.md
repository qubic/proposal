# Proposal to Include the QNFT Smart Contract

## Proposal

Include **QNFT**, a smart contract for issuing, holding, trading and renting NFTs represented by native Qubic assets, in Qubic Core.

QNFT provides collections, fixed-supply editions, metadata management, batch operations, a marketplace, rental subscriptions and shareholder governance. Collections can contain unique items or multiple interchangeable copies of an item. Owners can transfer available units to other users or export their management to compatible Qubic contracts when the collection permits it.

The contract follows the quantity-based model of ERC-1155 through a native Qubic API. It is not an Ethereum ABI implementation. Deployment includes a read-only QPI iterator for discovering an account's assets and a QPI safeguard that rejects management transfers to errored contracts before calling them. No new native asset type or asset-ledger format is required.

## Available Options

- **Option 0: No — do not include QNFT.**
- **Option 1: Yes — include QNFT with the functionality and initial economics described in this proposal.**

## Why QNFT

QNFT connects collection metadata and application features to native assets. Ownership is recorded in Qubic's asset ledger, so an NFT's identity survives a change of managing contract. Wallets and applications can retrieve its collection, metadata, supply and holders from QNFT.

One item can represent a unique artwork, a limited edition, a membership or a game item. Owners can sell copies through the built-in shop or grant paid, temporary usage rights while retaining ownership. Applications decide which benefits those rights provide.

Where collection policy permits, an owner can export part of an edition to an accepting contract, such as QX, while keeping the remaining units under QNFT management. External contracts apply their own rules and fees. Export does not transfer artwork files or require the receiving application to display them.

## Collections, Issuance and Ownership

The account creating a collection becomes its immutable creator. The administrator is initially unset. The creator can manage the collection directly or nominate an administrator, who must accept the role.

The creator and current administrator can mint, manage mutable metadata, pause or close minting, change the collection's external-transfer policy and nominate a replacement administrator. Replacing the administrator does not remove the creator's authority. Collection management does not grant permission to spend another owner's NFTs.

Collection limits on the number of item types and cumulative issued units are independently optional: zero means no creator-imposed limit. Positive limits are fixed at collection creation. Contract storage and monetary limits still apply.

QNFT supports three issuance patterns:

- **Mint:** create one item with a specified number of copies.
- **MintBatch:** create up to two independently described items in one request.
- **MultiMint:** create up to sixteen distinct items using shared metadata and settings, with a separate quantity for each item.

An item has one native asset identity. Its copies share metadata and have no individual serial numbers. For new editions, QNFT is the asset issuer; the collection creator is recorded separately, including when an administrator performs the mint. The recipient is specified explicitly.

QNFT does not mint additional units for an existing item. Owners can burn their units; burning reduces outstanding supply without restoring collection issuance quotas. Minting can be paused and resumed, or closed permanently. Closing minting does not stop trading or freeze mutable metadata.

Issued units that cannot immediately reach their recorded recipient remain reserved. QNFT retries delivery automatically on subsequent ticks without charging another mint fee.

### NFTs Using Existing Native Assets

A project can add an existing native asset to a collection through `RegisterAsset`. The registering account must be both the asset issuer and either the collection creator or its current administrator. It does not need to own any units. Registration attaches metadata and payout terms to the existing asset without issuing tokens or changing their owners.

For example, registering an existing issuance of one billion Punks creates one collection item with shared metadata. A holder of 1,000 Punks is shown as owning 1,000 copies of that item. All holders are recognized through the native ledger without registering separately or exchanging their tokens. The copies remain interchangeable and do not acquire separate images or serial numbers.

The collection must be open for issuance, and the asset must have zero decimal precision and at least one remaining unit. Smart-contract IPO shares are not eligible. Registration costs the current mint fee and uses one item slot in the collection and contract registry. It does not consume the collection's quota for newly issued units. Rejected registrations do not consume fees or quotas.

Units managed by QX or another contract remain there and appear in QNFT balance queries. To sell or rent available copies through QNFT, the holder voluntarily transfers management of the desired quantity to QNFT. Operations in other contracts remain subject to those contracts' rules and fees.

Registered items follow the same metadata, royalty and sale-dividend rules as newly minted items. The immutable collection creator receives the creator's rental allocation, even when the asset issuer is a different account. Existing owner-wide operator approvals also apply once the units are under QNFT management.

The association between the asset and its collection is permanent, including after all units are burned. An asset cannot be unregistered or moved to another collection. If its issuer later reissues the same asset name after native-ledger cleanup, QNFT treats it as the same item and preserves its metadata, payout terms and operator approvals. The new issuance may have a different quantity or decimal precision, so registration does not guarantee permanent scarcity. Balance and supply queries reflect the current native ledger.

## Transfers and Operator Permissions

Owners can transfer or burn individual quantities or use batches of up to sixteen entries. Through `SetApprovalForAll`, an owner can authorize multiple operators independently. Each approval covers the owner's current and future QNFT-managed holdings until revoked.

An operator can transfer, burn or export available units on the owner's behalf. Approval does not bypass collection restrictions or rental reservations, and does not authorize listing the owner's NFTs for sale or offering them for rent.

Transfers to smart contracts are subject to QPI call-order restrictions and the collection policy. Direct contract recipients must have a higher contract index than QNFT. Export changes asset management through the existing QPI acceptance mechanism; it can move only part of an edition and preserves ownership.

The creator or administrator can disable export and direct transfers to external smart contracts for the entire collection. This policy can be changed after minting and applies to existing and future units under QNFT control. It cannot recall or restrict units already exported. User transfers and the internal marketplace remain available.

Batch requests validate their inputs before execution. If a later execution step fails, completed entries can remain committed. Results identify the completed prefix and failure, so clients must not blindly replay the entire request. Native transfers do not provide Ethereum receiver callbacks or EVM-style atomic rollback.

## Metadata

An item can start with immutable or mutable metadata. For mutable items, the collection creator or administrator can update the URI and content hash repeatedly, then optionally freeze them permanently. These permissions remain subject to the item's policy even after a sale or administrator change.

Publishing final artwork after mint is supported by minting with placeholder metadata and updating it later. Updating does not automatically freeze the item. URI templates can use the ERC-1155 `{id}` convention, and QNFT provides a helper to resolve it.

QNFT stores references and hashes. Metadata documents and media remain in external storage, such as IPFS. QNFT does not fetch those files or verify their contents; creators and applications remain responsible for availability and interpretation.

## Built-in Marketplace

Owners can list available units at a fixed QU price. Listed units enter QNFT escrow. Buyers can purchase all or part of a lot, and the seller can cancel the unsold remainder. Changes to item metadata invalidate the listing's recorded metadata revision, protecting buyers from purchasing against stale terms.

The initial shop commission is **0.3% of the gross purchase price**. Optional royalties and sale dividends are calculated separately from the same gross price. The seller receives the remaining amount.

For a sale of 10,000 QU with a 5% royalty and no sale dividend, QNFT receives 30 QU, the royalty recipient receives 500 QU and the seller receives 9,470 QU.

Royalty terms are set when an item is minted or an existing asset is registered. A missing recipient or zero rate disables the royalty. `RoyaltyInfo` exposes a percentage-based quote following EIP-2981's calculation model for applications that choose to support it. It does not compel other marketplaces to pay.

Failed royalty or sale-dividend payments remain recorded as beneficiary credits and are retried automatically. QNFT does not enforce its commission, royalties or sale dividends on trades executed outside its marketplace.

### Optional Sale Dividends

At mint or registration, an item can designate an existing Qubic smart contract's IPO shares and a percentage of future QNFT shop sales. The selected asset must be issued by the zero identity and have 676 shares. Arbitrary user-issued tokens are not eligible. The selected shares and percentage are immutable item properties.

Each purchase distributes an equal whole-QU amount per share to the current possessors, including shares held under other managers. QNFT consolidates multiple records belonging to the same recipient. The allocation is separate from QNFT's own epoch dividends and does not apply to rentals.

The dividend budget is rounded down from the gross price, then divided into 676 equal whole-QU payments. Any indivisible remainder stays with the seller. For a 1,000,000 QU purchase with a 10% sale dividend, a 5% royalty and the initial 0.3% shop fee:

| Recipient | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Selected share possessors | 99,372 QU, or 147 QU per share |
| Royalty recipient | 50,000 QU |
| QNFT protocol | 3,000 QU |
| Seller | 847,628 QU |

The combined shop fee, royalty and sale-dividend rates must not exceed 100%. This is checked again when listing and purchasing. A later shop-fee increase can therefore prevent a listing from being purchased; cancellation remains available. `DividendInfo` exposes the configured shares, rate and calculated allocation.

## NFT Rentals

Rentals grant **usage rights only**. Ownership and possession remain with the lender. A renter cannot sell, transfer, burn or export units by virtue of renting them.

The owner creates an offer specifying available quantity, a QU price per unit per day, and a fixed billing period of at least 15 days. Renters can take multiple units, and different renters can take different units from an offer. Each unit can support only one active rental at a time.

Offered units and active rented units are reserved against sale, transfer, burn and export. The lender can cancel an offer's unpaid remainder, but cannot revoke access already paid for. Unreserved copies of the same item remain usable.

The first period is paid when the rental is accepted. Any excess payment becomes that rental's deposit. At a renewal boundary, QNFT uses the deposit to pay a full next period if automatic renewal remains enabled. It does not charge before the boundary or debit the renter's wallet. Insufficient funds end the rental; a later deposit cannot revive it.

Either party can disable renewal at any time. This preserves access until the paid period ends and cannot be reversed for that rental. The renter can top up or withdraw unused deposit after settling any elapsed periods. Paid rent is not refundable.

The initial rental payment split is:

| Recipient | Initial allocation |
| --- | ---: |
| Lender, the current NFT owner | 90% |
| QNFT protocol | 7% |
| Immutable collection creator | 3% |

Protocol and creator portions round down to whole QU; the lender receives the remainder. Changing the collection administrator does not redirect the creator's share. Failed rental payouts remain owed and are retried.

The protocol and creator rental rates are governed by QNFT shareholders. Current rates apply to new rentals and renewal periods when calculated, including elapsed periods not yet settled. Credits already recorded are unchanged.

Applications can query an account's owned NFTs and active incoming rentals, including quantities, lender and expiry. Ownership and rental access are reported separately. Applications must enforce the benefits they associate with those rights. Queries account for elapsed rental periods even if tick processing has not yet updated storage.

## Initial Fees

Amounts below are in QU. Mint fees apply per newly issued item, not per copy. Batch transfers charge per completed entry; batch minting charges per issued item.

| Operation | Initial fee |
| --- | ---: |
| Create a collection | 1,000,000 QU |
| Mint an item, including a multi-copy edition | 100,000 QU |
| Register an existing native asset | 100,000 QU, following the item minting fee |
| Nominate a collection administrator | 10,000 QU |
| Transfer NFT units | 10,000 QU |
| Grant an operator approval | 10,000 QU |
| Update mutable metadata | 50,000 QU |
| Export asset management | 10,000 QU, plus any destination charge |
| Create a shop listing | 10,000 QU |
| Buy through the shop | 0.3% of the purchase price |
| Rent NFT units | 7% protocol allocation and 3% creator allocation from paid rent |
| Submit or replace a shareholder proposal | 1,000,000 QU |

There is no separate QNFT fee for revoking an operator, burning units, cancelling a listing, accepting an administrator nomination, freezing metadata, changing minting status or the external-transfer policy, or casting shareholder votes. Rental offer creation and cancellation, deposit management and renewal cancellation have no separate fixed fee. Cancelling a shareholder proposal is also free.

At initial rates, creating a collection and minting an edition of 100 identical copies costs **1,100,000 QU**. Creating a collection and minting 100 distinct items costs **11,000,000 QU**.

Excess payment is refunded, except where it intentionally funds a rental deposit or the execution reserve. Rejected requests return refundable attached funds through the normal refund mechanism. A charge already consumed by a destination contract cannot be refunded again. Failed refunds remain recorded for automatic retry.

## Revenue Distribution

QNFT protocol fees, including its rental allocation, accumulate during the epoch and are distributed at its end:

| Recipient or purpose | Initial allocation |
| --- | ---: |
| Developer | 20% |
| QNFT shareholders | 60% |
| Burn into the contract execution reserve | 20% |

Seller proceeds, royalty and sale-dividend obligations, lender and creator rental credits, unused rental deposits and refundable user funds are excluded from this revenue pool. A sale dividend attributable to shares possessed by QNFT itself becomes protocol revenue.

The developer receives a direct payment. QNFT shareholders receive dividends through Qubic's distribution mechanism. The burn allocation replenishes the execution reserve. Dividend rounding remainders are carried forward, and failed payments remain accounted for without paying successful portions twice.

## Shareholder Governance

Voting rights come from QNFT contract shares, independently of NFT ownership. Shareholders can propose individual or grouped changes to these parameters:

| Parameter | Initial value |
| --- | ---: |
| Collection creation fee | 1,000,000 QU |
| Item minting fee | 100,000 QU |
| Shop commission | 0.3% |
| Shareholder revenue allocation | 60% |
| Execution-reserve burn allocation | 20% |
| Administrator nomination fee | 10,000 QU |
| Transfer fee | 10,000 QU |
| Operator approval fee | 10,000 QU |
| Metadata update fee | 50,000 QU |
| Export fee | 10,000 QU |
| Listing fee | 10,000 QU |
| Proposal submission fee | 1,000,000 QU |
| Rental protocol allocation | 7% |
| Rental creator allocation | 3% |

Accepted changes take effect after the closing epoch's revenue distribution. **The developer allocation remains fixed at 20%.** Shareholder and execution-reserve allocations must together account for the remaining 80%.

The rental protocol and creator rates may each range from 0% to 100%, but their sum must not exceed 100%. The lender receives the remainder. These rates can be rebalanced together in a batch proposal.

Fixed fees may be zero or any supported monetary amount. The shop rate may range from 0% to 100%, subject to each sale's combined-allocation checks. Applications should read current fees and rental terms before submitting transactions. Initial economics are policy choices, not NFT-standard requirements or a promise of profitability.

## Standards and Native Qubic Adaptation

QNFT adopts the multi-token model of **ERC-1155** and the royalty-information calculation of **ERC-2981**, also known as **EIP-2981**. These are native QPI adaptations, not claims of full Ethereum interface compliance.

### ERC-1155: Token Types, Balances and Transfers

ERC-1155 defines balances by account and token ID, owner-wide operator approvals, single and batch safe transfers, events and interface discovery. Its optional metadata extension defines URI access and the `{id}` convention. Full compliance includes receiver checks and failure semantics, not just matching method names. [ERC-1155 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1155)

| Capability | Current QNFT implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Multiple token types and quantities | Each item ID identifies a native asset. A quantity of one represents a unique item; larger quantities represent interchangeable copies. |
| Single and batch balance queries | `BalanceOf` and `BalanceOfBatch` read native ownership, including units managed outside QNFT. |
| Owner-wide operator approvals | `SetApprovalForAll` and `IsApprovedForAll` cover current and future QNFT-managed holdings. |
| Single and batch transfers | `TransferFrom` and `BatchTransfer` check owner/operator authority and available quantity. Native restrictions and reservations also apply. |
| Metadata URI | `URI` returns the stored reference; `ResolvedURI` expands `{id}` into 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits. Off-chain JSON interpretation remains the client's responsibility. |
| Transfer, approval and metadata notifications | Native logs and journal entries record QNFT operations. Batch movements emit individual `TransferSingle` records rather than one aggregate `TransferBatch` record. |

The following differences prevent full ERC-1155 compliance:

- **No Ethereum ABI or ERC-165 discovery.** QNFT uses Qubic identities, native numeric types and registered QPI entry points. `GetProtocolInfo` describes the native version and encoding limits; it does not implement `supportsInterface`.
- **No ERC-1155 receiver handshake or arbitrary data forwarding.** QNFT does not call `onERC1155Received` or `onERC1155BatchReceived`. The current QPI integration has no equivalent standardized receiver protocol; contract calls also obey Qubic's index ordering. Native transfers are therefore not named `safeTransferFrom` or `safeBatchTransferFrom`.
- **No general atomic batch rollback.** Current QPI asset operations can commit before a later operation fails. QNFT reports partial completion instead of promising an EVM revert of the whole batch. Adding read-only QPI helpers cannot supply that rollback guarantee.
- **Native events are not Ethereum logs.** External managers can move exported assets without invoking QNFT. QNFT's journal alone cannot reconstruct every subsequent movement; consumers must also use native ledger queries.
- **Bounded inputs and fixed issuance.** Requests use fixed-size arrays, item IDs and quantities have native limits, and existing item types cannot receive additional issuance. These reflect QNFT's storage and native asset model.

Collection roles, mint APIs, burn APIs, optional issuance limits, mint closure, metadata freezing, rentals and sale dividends are QNFT application features. ERC-1155 does not prescribe these management APIs or require a particular creator/administrator policy. QNFT's fixed-supply choice is not an ERC-1155 requirement.

### ERC-2981: Royalty Information

ERC-2981 defines a query for the recipient and royalty amount for a supplied sale price. The amount must be percentage-based and denominated in the same units as that price; integer rounding is permitted. It does not itself execute payment or force every marketplace to pay. [ERC-2981 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981)

QNFT implements that calculation through `RoyaltyInfo(itemId, salePrice)`:

- Returns the configured Qubic recipient and `floor(salePrice * royaltyBps / 10000)`.
- Uses the full supplied price, without subtracting shop fees or sale dividends.
- Makes no assumption that the quote is denominated in QU; the caller chooses the monetary units. The built-in shop separately settles its purchases in QU.
- Returns zero royalty when the recipient or effective rate is absent. The quote remains available after export while the item has positive supply.
- Performs no payment or state change. Negative prices are rejected; unknown or fully burned items return `NotFound`.

QNFT does not expose Ethereum's `royaltyInfo(uint256,uint256)` ABI, Ethereum recipient addresses or ERC-165 royalty-interface discovery. It uses a native result structure with a status code and a nonnegative signed 64-bit price range. An Ethereum integration would need its own interface and recipient mapping.

The built-in shop pays configured royalties as part of its purchase logic. External marketplaces may query and honor them, but QNFT cannot force payment after export. This enforcement boundary is separate from royalty discovery. `DividendInfo` and the rental creator allocation are QNFT extensions, not ERC-2981 royalty outputs.

## Integration Scope

QNFT provides native balance, supply, metadata, royalty, dividend, holder, marketplace and rental queries, with pagination and an event journal for consumers. Ledger-based queries reflect externally managed assets; transfer and rental operations require units that QNFT can control.

This proposal covers the native Qubic contract and its required QPI changes. It does not activate an Ethereum bridge or provide a QBay migration route. Ethereum marketplace compatibility would require a separate representation and a verified bridge mechanism.
